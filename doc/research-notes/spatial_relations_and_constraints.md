# Symbolic spatial relations and constraint-based layout: theory for onionskin

Notation used throughout. Two boxes A and B. On one axis A is the interval [a1, a2] and B is [b1, b2], with a1 < a2 and b1 < b2. On the x axis a1 is the left edge and a2 the right edge. On the y axis of a browser, y grows downwards, so "before" on y means "above".

Verification labels used below:
- [PR] peer-reviewed journal or conference paper. [not PR] technical report, preprint, vendor documentation or web page.
- "metadata verified" means title, authors, venue, year, pages and DOI were checked against the Crossref API (api.crossref.org) in this session. "content verified" means the claim was read in the source (abstract or full text) in this session. Claims marked "derived" are worked out from the definitions in these notes and are not quoted from any source.

## 1. Allen's interval algebra and how it applies to one axis of a layout

### Takeaway
Allen's 13 basic relations are jointly exhaustive and pairwise disjoint (JEPD) for intervals of positive length, and each is a conjunction of comparisons between the two intervals' endpoints. Applied to the x or y projection of two boxes, they give a complete, exact per-axis vocabulary (left-of with a gap, touching, overlapping, left-aligned, centred within, and so on), and a lattice structure due to Ligozat makes coarse relations easy to define and compute.

### Cited Findings
- Source: James F. Allen, "Maintaining knowledge about temporal intervals", Communications of the ACM 26(11):832-843, 1983. [PR], metadata verified. [doi:10.1145/182.358434](https://doi.org/10.1145/182.358434)
- Allen's algebra has 13 basic relations between intervals: before (p), meets (m), overlaps (o), starts (s), during (d), finishes (f), equals (eq) and the inverses of the six that are not symmetric (pi, mi, oi, si, di, fi). The 13 symbols are listed, with their lattice positions, in the OGRE project report (Bécher, Enjalbert, Fievé, Gosselin, Lévy, Ligozat, "Rapport technique du projet OGRE", CNRS, 2005, [not PR], arXiv cs/0610004), content verified. [arXiv cs/0610004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0610004); original: [Allen 1983](https://doi.org/10.1145/182.358434)
- Ligozat's encoding puts the 13 relations on a lattice indexed by integer pairs from 0 to 4. Convex relations are the intervals [a, b] of this lattice. They are exactly the disjunctions of basic relations that can be written as a conjunction of endpoint constraints without "not equal" (example given: {p, m} is convex because it is X2 <= Y1). Content verified in the OGRE report, section II.1, which credits the result to G. Ligozat. [arXiv cs/0610004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0610004)
- The composition of two convex relations is convex and needs only two table lookups: [a, b] o [c, d] = [inf(a o c), sup(b o d)]. Content verified, OGRE report section II.2. [arXiv cs/0610004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0610004)
- Pre-convex relations are obtained from a convex relation by removing relations other than p, o, d, di, oi, pi. They coincide with the ORD-Horn class, which Nebel and Bürckert showed is the unique maximal tractable class (containing all basic relations). Content verified in the OGRE report section II.1; primary source: Bernhard Nebel and Hans-Jürgen Bürckert, "Reasoning about temporal relations: a maximal tractable subclass of Allen's interval algebra", Journal of the ACM 42(1):43-66, 1995, [PR], metadata verified. [doi:10.1145/200836.200848](https://doi.org/10.1145/200836.200848); [arXiv cs/0610004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0610004)
- The tractable subalgebras of Allen's algebra were fully classified by Andrei Krokhin, Peter Jeavons and Peter Jonsson, "Reasoning about temporal relations: the tractable subalgebras of Allen's interval algebra", Journal of the ACM 50(5):591-640, 2003. [PR], metadata verified only. [doi:10.1145/876638.876639](https://doi.org/10.1145/876638.876639)
- Survey: Anthony G. Cohn and Jochen Renz, "Qualitative spatial representation and reasoning", chapter 13 in Handbook of Knowledge Representation (Foundations of Artificial Intelligence vol. 3), Elsevier, 2008, pp. 551-596. [PR, edited handbook], metadata verified, content not re-read in this session. [doi:10.1016/S1574-6526(07)03013-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1574-6526(07)03013-1)
- Book: Gérard Ligozat, "Qualitative Spatial and Temporal Reasoning", ISTE/Wiley. Crossref gives 2013 (the Wiley online edition) while the print edition is commonly cited as 2011. [not a peer-reviewed article; scholarly monograph], metadata verified. [doi:10.1002/9781118601457](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118601457)

Table of the 13 relations, derived from the endpoint definitions. "Zone code" is the Ligozat-style pair (zone of a1, zone of a2), where the zones relative to B are 0 = before b1, 1 = at b1, 2 = strictly between b1 and b2, 3 = at b2, 4 = after b2.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | p | a2 < b1 | (0,0) | A left of B, with a gap | A above B, with a gap |
| meets | m | a2 = b1 | (0,1) | A's right edge touches B's left edge | A's bottom touches B's top |
| overlaps | o | a1 < b1 < a2 < b2 | (0,2) | A overlaps B's left part | A overlaps B's top part |
| finished-by | fi | a1 < b1, a2 = b2 | (0,3) | right edges aligned, A starts further left | bottoms aligned, A taller upwards |
| contains | di | a1 < b1, b2 < a2 | (0,4) | A sticks out on both sides of B | A sticks out above and below B |
| starts | s | a1 = b1, a2 < b2 | (1,2) | left edges aligned, A narrower | tops aligned, A shorter |
| equals | eq | a1 = b1, a2 = b2 | (1,3) | same horizontal extent | same vertical extent |
| started-by | si | a1 = b1, b2 < a2 | (1,4) | left edges aligned, A wider | tops aligned, A taller |
| during | d | b1 < a1, a2 < b2 | (2,2) | A strictly within B's horizontal span | A strictly within B's vertical span |
| finishes | f | b1 < a1, a2 = b2 | (2,3) | right edges aligned, A narrower | bottoms aligned, A shorter |
| overlapped-by | oi | b1 < a1 < b2 < a2 | (2,4) | A overlaps B's right part | A overlaps B's bottom part |
| met-by | mi | a1 = b2 | (3,4) | A's left edge touches B's right edge | A's top touches B's bottom |
| after | pi | b2 < a1 | (4,4) | A right of B, with a gap | A below B, with a gap |

### Inferences
- (Derived) The valid zone codes are exactly the pairs (i, j) with i <= j, excluding (1,1) and (3,3), which would need a zero-length A. That gives 15 - 2 = 13. The lattice order is component-wise order on the codes. So a per-axis relation can be computed from four endpoint comparisons (a1 vs b1, a1 vs b2, a2 vs b1, a2 vs b2) and stored as two small integers.
- (Derived) Allen's relations need positive-length intervals. Browser boxes can have zero width or height (empty elements, collapsed margins, hidden content). For these onionskin needs either point-to-interval relations (a point has 5 positions relative to an interval: before, at start, inside, at end, after; this follows from the zone definition) or Ligozat's generalised intervals, studied for example in P. Balbiani, J.-F. Condotta, L. Fariñas del Cerro and A. Osmani, "Reasoning about generalized intervals", LNCS (AIMSA 1998), pp. 50-61 ([PR], metadata verified only, [doi:10.1007/BFb0057434](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0057434)).
- (Derived) Allen's relations say nothing about centres or sizes. "Centred on B" and "same width as B" are not expressible as unions of Allen relations; see section 6 for adding centre points and size comparisons.
- For a test library the reasoning results (composition, tractability) matter less than the vocabulary, because onionskin computes relations from measured boxes rather than inferring them. Composition is still useful for explaining failures and for checking that a set of expectations is not contradictory before running it.

### Gaps
- Allen's original paper was not re-read in this session; the relation list and endpoint definitions are standard and were cross-checked against the OGRE report's lattice figure, whose extracted text was partly garbled.
- The commonly cited counts of 82 convex relations and 868 ORD-Horn relations were not verified in this session.

## 2. Rectangle algebra: 169 relations and how to coarsen them

### Takeaway
The rectangle algebra (RA) takes one Allen relation per axis, giving 13 x 13 = 169 JEPD basic relations between axis-aligned rectangles. It is the natural fine-grained base for onionskin. Its tractable fragment (strongly preconvex relations) was shown in 2024 to be maximal, which supports building coarse relations as per-axis lattice intervals.

### Cited Findings
- The basic rectangle relation between two rectangles is the pair of the Allen relation between their x projections and the Allen relation between their y projections, written alpha (x) beta; the set of these is the 169 basic relations. RA relations extend to arbitrary bounded regions via their minimum bounding rectangles. Content verified in Weiming Liu, Xiaotong Zhang, Sanjiang Li and Mingsheng Ying, "Reasoning about cardinal directions between extended objects", Artificial Intelligence 174(12-13):951-983, 2010, [PR], metadata verified; read via the arXiv version 0909.0138. [not PR], existence and report number verified via secondary pages only; full text not found (the ICSI server refused connections). ### Inferences
- (Derived) Because RA is a product, a 2D relation is just a pair [x-rel y-rel]. onionskin should expose the pair as data rather than give names to 169 relations. Names belong to coarser sets.
- (Derived) Every coarse 2D relation used below (directions, topology, alignment) is a union of RA basic relations, and every one depends on (x-rel, y-rel) alone. So the library can compute the finest relation once per pair of boxes and answer any coarse question by set membership. This makes the levels consistent by construction: a box pair satisfies a coarse relation exactly when its fine relation is a member.
- (Derived) Coarsening 13 to 6 per axis by "which of B's three bands does A cover with positive length" gives: {p, m} -> before-band only; {s, d, f, eq} -> middle band only; {mi, pi} -> after-band only; {o, fi} -> before + middle; {oi, si} -> middle + after; {di} -> all three. The product, 6 x 6 = 36, is the number of sub-rectangles of a 3 x 3 grid, which matches the size of the rectangular cardinal relations studied by Isabel Navarrete, Antonio Morales, Guido Sciavicco and M. Antonia Cárdenas-Viedma, "Spatial reasoning with rectangular cardinal relations", Annals of Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence 67(1):31-70, 2013 ([PR], metadata verified only, [doi:10.1007/s10472-012-9327-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10472-012-9327-5)). The count 36 is derived here, not read in the paper.
- (Derived) Coarsening 13 to 3 per axis ({p, m} before; the 9 relations [o, oi] overlapping; {mi, pi} after) gives 3 x 3 = 9 JEPD relations: the eight compass directions plus "projections overlap on both axes". This is the box version of Frank's projection-based cardinal directions (section 3).

### Gaps
- The KR 1998 rectangle algebra paper and Guesgen's report were not read. Whether Mukerjee and Joe used exactly 13 relations per axis could not be confirmed.
- The IJCAI-99 abstract was read only through a search-result summary; the precise definition of "strongly preconvex" was not checked.

## 3. Coarser calculi, conceptual neighbourhoods and granularity

### Takeaway
Three established coarse calculi map onto boxes: RCC-8 (topology: disjoint, touching, overlapping, inside, equal), cardinal directions (compass zones, with the 3 x 3 direction-relation matrix), and Freksa's semi-interval "coarse relations", which are single or paired edge comparisons and are all convex lattice intervals. Freksa's conceptual neighbourhoods give a principled notion of a "near miss" between relations, and Euzenat formalised how relations change when granularity changes.

### Inferences
- (Derived) Each of Freksa's coarse relations is one or two edge comparisons. Working from the lattice intervals above: older [p, di] is a1 < b1; younger [d, pi] is a1 > b1; head to head [s, si] is a1 = b1; tail to tail [fi, f] is a2 = b2; survives [di, pi] is a2 > b2; survived by [p, d] is a2 < b2; precedes [p, m] is a2 <= b1; succeeds [mi, pi] is a1 >= b2; contemporary [o, oi] is a1 < b2 and b1 < a2 (interiors overlap); [o, pi] is a2 > b1; [p, oi] is a1 < b2. In layout words on the x axis: "left-aligned" = head to head, "right-aligned" = tail to tail, "left of (touching allowed)" = precedes, "horizontally overlapping" = contemporary, "starts further left" = older. This is the strongest theoretical support for onionskin's coarse vocabulary: named relations are edge comparisons, and each named relation is a convex lattice interval.
- (Derived) Under these endpoint readings, [o, pi] (a2 > b1) fits "X died after Y's birth" and [p, oi] (a1 < b2) fits "X was born before Y's death", which is the reverse of the name assignment in the OGRE table. Likewise [oi, pi] (a1 > b1 and a2 > b2) reads as "younger and survives", not "younger and survived by". The OGRE table may contain slips; onionskin should define its names by edge conditions, not by Freksa's labels.
- (Derived) RCC-8 on boxes is a function of the RA pair (x-rel, y-rel), treating boxes as closed regions:
  - DC (disjoint): x-rel or y-rel in {p, pi}.
  - EC (touching): not DC, and x-rel or y-rel in {m, mi}. If the other axis is in [o, oi] the boxes share an edge segment; if the other axis is also in {m, mi} they touch only at a corner. onionskin may want both "touching" and "corner-touching".
  - Otherwise both axes are in [o, oi] and the interiors overlap. EQ: both eq. NTPP (strictly inside): both d. TPP (inside, touching the boundary): both in {s, d, f, eq}, not both d, not both eq. NTPPi and TPPi: the same with {si, di, fi, eq}. PO (partial overlap): everything else.
  - RCC-8 is JEPD, and this mapping is total and single-valued, so it is a clean coarsening of the 169.
- (Derived) The CDC variant for disconnected regions (511 relations) suits elements that render as several rectangles, such as an inline element broken over lines (several client rects). The primary object can be the union of its fragments while the reference object is its bounding box, which is exactly CDC's asymmetric design.
- (Derived) Freksa's conceptual neighbourhood gives a principled "how close was it" for failure messages: if the expected relation is meets and the observed is overlaps, they are neighbours (moving one edge a little changes one into the other). A failure report can name the observed relation and the edge that has to move, and by how much.
- (Derived) Tolerance can be read as a change of granularity in Euzenat's sense: a gap smaller than the tolerance turns "before" into "meets" at the coarser grain. His six constraints on conversion operators may be a useful checklist for designing onionskin's coarsening functions, but this was not checked against his actual operator tables.

## 4. Tolerance and indeterminate boundaries ("almost meets", "approximately aligned")

### Takeaway
The literature models tolerance in three ways: thick boundaries (Aiello and Smeulders, for document images), fuzzy Allen relations (Schockaert, De Cock and Kerre; Badaloni and Giacomin), and rough or indeterminate boundaries (Bittner and Stell, Roy and Stell). For a test library the simplest sound choice is a crisp three-way comparison with an epsilon at each edge comparison. It keeps the 13 Allen relations JEPD as long as both intervals are longer than 2 x epsilon, but "approximately equal" is then not transitive, which has consequences for chains of alignment.

### Cited Findings
- Marco Aiello and Arnold W. M. Smeulders, "Thick 2D relations for document understanding", Information Sciences 167(1-4):147-176, 2004. [PR], metadata verified. The paper uses a propositional language of bidimensional Allen (13 x 13) rectangle relations to detect reading order in document images, and introduces a "thick boundary interpretation" of the qualitative relations to make the system robust and avoid brittleness on real-life images; recall up to 89% on heterogeneous documents. ### Inferences
- (Derived) Define edge comparison with tolerance e >= 0: x "is before" y if x < y - e; x "is at" y if |x - y| <= e; x "is after" y if x > y + e. Exactly one holds for any two numbers, so each comparison is JEPD.
- (Derived) Put these into Allen's endpoint definitions to get tolerant Allen relations. If both intervals are longer than 2e, an endpoint of A can be "at" at most one endpoint of B (otherwise b2 - b1 <= 2e), so the five zones (before b1, at b1, between, at b2, after b2) are disjoint and cover the line, and the same argument rules out codes (1,1) and (3,3). So the tolerant relations are again exactly the 13 and still JEPD. For a box narrower than 2e (a 1 px rule, an empty span), the tolerant comparisons can give combinations outside the 13, so onionskin should switch that box to point semantics (5 zones) or reject the assertion with a clear message.
- (Derived) With e > 0, "at" is reflexive and symmetric but not transitive: if A's left is within e of B's left and B's within e of C's, A and C may differ by up to 2e. This is the semiorder situation Luce analysed. Consequences: "A, B and C are left-aligned" should be checked as spread (max left edge minus min left edge <= e), not as a chain of pairwise checks; and the Allen composition table is not sound for tolerant relations, so onionskin should not infer relations transitively from tolerant ones.
- (Derived) The thick-boundary approach and the epsilon comparison above are the same idea in crisp form. Fuzzy relations give a degree in [0, 1] instead of a yes/no; that suits ranking or diagnostics ("how nearly aligned"), but a test needs a pass/fail cut, which reduces to a crisp threshold again. A reasonable design is crisp pass/fail with the measured deviation reported.
- (Derived) Tolerance should be one parameter per assertion (with a library default), applied to every edge comparison of that assertion, so that coarse and fine assertions with the same epsilon agree: if the fine relation computed with e is in the coarse set, the coarse assertion with e passes.

## 6. Constraint-based layout specification (Cassowary, constraint hierarchies, CCSS, Auto Layout)

### Takeaway
Constraint layout systems express layout as linear equalities and inequalities between edge, centre and size attributes, with priorities separating required from preferred constraints. Apple's Auto Layout gives a compact, widely used vocabulary: one attribute of one item relates (=, <=, >=) to a multiple of one attribute of another item plus a constant, with strict rules about which attributes may be related. This form covers every quantitative expectation onionskin needs ("gap between 4 and 8 px", "same width as", "half the width of").

### Cited Findings
- Greg J. Badros, Alan Borning and Peter J. Stuckey, "The Cassowary linear arithmetic constraint solving algorithm", ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction 8(4):267-306, 2001. [PR], metadata and abstract verified. Linear equalities and inequalities arise naturally in UI specification, for example requiring one window to be left of another, requiring a pane to occupy the leftmost third of a window, or preferring that an object be contained in a rectangle if possible. Cassowary is an incremental algorithm based on the dual simplex method that solves such systems efficiently. [doi:10.1145/504704.504705](https://doi.org/10.1145/504704.504705)
- Alan Borning, Bjorn Freeman-Benson and Molly Wilson, "Constraint hierarchies", Lisp and Symbolic Computation 5(3):223-270, 1992. [PR], metadata verified; content not re-read (the required/strong/medium/weak hierarchy and the comparators for choosing among solutions are from this line of work). [doi:10.1007/BF01807506](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01807506)
- Greg J. Badros, Alan Borning, Kim Marriott and Peter Stuckey, "Constraint cascading style sheets for the Web", UIST 1999, pp. 73-82. [PR], metadata verified only. [doi:10.1145/320719.322588](https://doi.org/10.1145/320719.322588). Note: the DOI 10.1145/320719.322581 sometimes given for this paper resolves to a different UIST 1999 paper (PeopleGarden).
- Related: Alan Borning, Richard Kuang-Hsu Lin and Kim Marriott, "Constraint-based document layout for the Web", Multimedia Systems 8(3):177-189, 2000 ([PR], metadata verified, [doi:10.1007/s005300000043](https://doi.org/10.1007/s005300000043)); Greg J. Badros et al., "A constraint extension to scalable vector graphics", WWW 2001, pp. 489-498 ([PR], metadata verified, [doi:10.1145/371920.372146](https://doi.org/10.1145/371920.372146)); Hiroshi Hosobe, "A modular geometric constraint solver for user interface applications", UIST 2001, pp. 91-100 ([PR], metadata verified, [doi:10.1145/502348.502362](https://doi.org/10.1145/502348.502362)).
- Apple Auto Layout, "Anatomy of a Constraint", Auto Layout Guide (archived documentation). [not PR], content verified. [Apple developer archive](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/AutolayoutPG/AnatomyofaConstraint.html). Points read on the page:
  - Each constraint is one linear equation: item1.attribute1 = multiplier x item2.attribute2 + constant. Item 2 may be empty, with attribute 2 "Not an Attribute" and multiplier 0, for constant constraints such as height = 40. The equation states equality, not assignment.
  - The relation is one of =, >=, <=. One equality can be replaced by two inequalities, but two inequalities are not always equivalent to one equality.
  - Attributes: leading, trailing, top, bottom, left, right (edges); width, height (size); center X, center Y; one or more baselines for text. The page advises leading and trailing over left and right so layouts follow reading direction.
  - Rules: a size attribute cannot be constrained to a location attribute; constants cannot be assigned to location attributes alone; location attributes cannot use a multiplier other than 1.0; vertical location attributes cannot be constrained to horizontal ones; leading/trailing cannot be mixed with left/right. Aspect ratio (height = k x own width) is the stated exception to the size/location split.
  - Priorities run from 1 to 1000; 1000 is required, others are optional. Optional constraints are satisfied in priority order; an unsatisfiable optional constraint is skipped but still pulls the layout towards the closest solution. Suggested levels cluster around 250 (low), 500, 750 (high) and 1000 (required). Example: a required >= 8 spacing plus an optional <= 8 at priority 250 keeps views at least 8 points apart and as close to 8 as possible.
  - Intrinsic content size enters as content hugging (width <= intrinsic width, default priority 250) and compression resistance (width >= intrinsic width, default priority 750).

### Inferences
- (Derived) Auto Layout's attribute set lines up with the theory: edges are Allen's endpoints (Freksa's semi-intervals), center X/Y are the midpoints Allen lacks, and width/height are the durations that INDU adds (see section 7). Its mixing rules are the same split onionskin needs: compare x-location with x-location, y with y, size with size.
- (Derived) Quantity expectations in onionskin can use exactly this form, read as a check instead of a constraint to solve: A.attr REL m x B.attr + c, REL in {=, <=, >=}, with = meaning |lhs - rhs| <= e. Examples: "gap between 4 and 8 px" is the pair B.left >= A.right + 4 and B.left <= A.right + 8; "same width" is A.width = 1.0 x B.width + 0 with tolerance e; "half the width" is A.width = 0.5 x B.width; "at least 40 px tall" is A.height >= 40. Every coarse relation in sections 1 to 3 is a conjunction of such checks with m = 1 and c = 0, so the symbolic and quantitative layers share one model and one tolerance rule.
- (Derived) "Roughly the same width" has two reasonable readings: absolute (|wA - wB| <= e) and relative (|wA / wB - 1| <= r). The multiplier form covers the relative reading as two inequalities: wA >= (1 - r) wB and wA <= (1 + r) wB.
- (Derived) Priorities map onto test severity: required = must hold (failure), optional = should hold (warning or soft report). Constraint hierarchy comparators (how to rank solutions that violate soft constraints) could inform how a report ranks several soft failures, but onionskin does not need a solver because it only checks measured values.
- (Derived) Logical versus physical edges: Apple recommends leading/trailing. For a test library that checks rendered output, physical left/right/top/bottom are the primary terms, with logical start/end variants useful for right-to-left tests.

## 7. Recommendation inputs: JEPD sets, a lattice of granularity levels, and consistent tolerance

### Takeaway
A principled design is: compute the finest relation (13 Allen relations per axis, plus size and centre comparisons) once per pair of boxes, with a single epsilon applied to every edge comparison; define every coarser relation as a set of fine relations; and only give names to coarse relations that are JEPD families (directions, RCC-8 topology) or convex lattice intervals (single edge comparisons such as left-aligned). This guarantees that levels agree with each other and that each failure can be explained as edge comparisons.

### Cited Findings
- JEPD base sets confirmed in the sources: Allen's 13 relations ([Allen 1983](https://doi.org/10.1145/182.358434)); the 169 RA relations as pairs of Allen relations ([Liu et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2010.05.006)); CDC's 218 direction-relation matrices for connected regions and 511 for disconnected regions ([Liu et al. 2010, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.0138)).
- Coarse relations defined by bound constraints are convex lattice intervals, and convex relations are closed under composition ([OGRE report](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0610004)). Ord-Horn (pre-convex) is the maximal tractable class for Allen ([Nebel and Bürckert 1995](https://doi.org/10.1145/200836.200848)) and for RA ([Bodirsky et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1534304)).
- Interval relations combined with relative duration: Arun K. Pujari, G. Vijaya Kumari and Abdul Sattar, "INDU: an interval and duration network", Advanced Topics in Artificial Intelligence (AI'99), LNCS 1747, pp. 291-303, 1999. [PR], metadata verified only. [doi:10.1007/3-540-46695-9_25](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-46695-9_25). Also S. Badaloni, M. Giacomin and C. Masolo, "Qualitative temporal representation and reasoning about points, intervals and durations", TIME 2001, pp. 51-56 ([PR], metadata verified only, [doi:10.1109/TIME.2001.930697](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIME.2001.930697)).
- Granularity conversion has formal constraints and unique operator pairs for temporal relations ([Euzenat 2001](https://doi.org/10.1111/0824-7935.00170)).
- Auto Layout's model (one attribute related to a multiple of another plus a constant, with =, <=, >= and priorities) is a proven practical vocabulary ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/AutolayoutPG/AnatomyofaConstraint.html)).

### Inferences
- (Derived) Per-axis primitives. For boxes A and B on one axis, compute with tolerance e the comparisons of A's {start, centre, end} with B's {start, centre, end} and of A's length with B's length. From the four start/end comparisons get the Allen relation (zone code). From the length comparison get narrower/same/wider. Combining Allen with length gives 25 consistent per-axis relations: s, d, f force A shorter; si, di, fi force A longer; eq forces equal; p, m, o and their inverses allow all three (6 x 3 + 7 = 25). This matches what INDU calls its basic relations, but the count 25 is derived here, not read in Pujari et al.
- (Derived) Proposed granularity levels. Each level's relations are unions of level-1 relations, so a computed level-1 relation determines all coarser answers.

| Level | Per axis | 2D | JEPD? | Typical assertion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0: metric | edge, centre, size values | values for both axes | n/a | gap in [4, 8] px; width = 0.5 x B.width +/- e |
| 1: basic | 13 Allen (25 with size) | 169 RA pairs (625 with sizes) | yes | x: meets, y: during |
| 2: coarse | 6 band-occupancy classes; named convex edge relations (left-aligned, right-aligned, centre-aligned, precedes, horizontally overlapping) | 36 rectangular cardinal; RCC-8 (8); alignment relations | families yes, named alignments no (they are single conditions, not partitions) | inside, touching, overlaps, left-aligned |
| 3: sketch | 3 (before, overlapping, after) | 9 (eight compass directions + overlapping) | yes | left-of, above, below-right |

- (Derived) Name only two kinds of coarse relation. (a) JEPD families, which partition all box pairs: 3-per-axis direction, 6-per-axis band occupancy, RCC-8, the 9 compass cells. Here the test can report "expected X, observed Y" with Y from the same family. (b) Single convex conditions, which are one or two edge comparisons: left-aligned (a1 at b1), right-aligned (a2 at b2), centred (midpoints at each other), left-of (a2 before or at b1), strictly-left-of with gap (a2 before b1), horizontally overlapping (a1 before b2 and b1 before a2). Convexity keeps each name expressible as a conjunction of anchor comparisons without "not equal", so it is easy to explain and to translate into the Auto Layout form.
- (Derived) Suggested mapping of the requested vocabulary:
  - left-of / right-of / above / below: x-rel (or y-rel) in {p, m} (or {mi, pi}); the 3-per-axis level. Optionally "strict" for p only (positive gap beyond e) and "flush" for m only.
  - inside: RCC-8 TPP, NTPP or EQ (both axes in {s, d, f, eq}); "strictly inside" = NTPP.
  - overlaps: interiors intersect (both axes in [o, oi]); "partially overlaps" = RCC-8 PO.
  - touching: RCC-8 EC, split into edge-touching and corner-touching.
  - aligned: edge or centre comparisons "at" within e (left, right, top, bottom, centre-x, centre-y, plus baseline if text baselines are captured).
  - same width / height: length comparison "at" within e, or relative tolerance r.
- (Derived) Tolerance rules for consistency: one e per assertion with a library default; e applies to every edge comparison inside the assertion; Allen-based relations require both lengths > 2e, else use point semantics or fail with a message; group alignment uses spread <= e, never chained pairwise checks; no transitive inference from tolerant relations. A default e of about half a CSS pixel to one device pixel seems reasonable for subpixel layout noise, but no source in this research supports a specific value.
- (Derived) Failure reports: give the observed level-1 relation, the expected set, and the edge comparisons that failed with their measured values. Conceptual neighbourhood can label near misses (observed relation adjacent to the expected set).
